Three ways to read one frame

The same colour negative on the same OpticFilm 8200i, read three ways: upstream SANE's genesys GL128, our plustek8200i, October, and our plustek8200i, today. The first two are the images published in the original two-driver comparison on 9 October 2026; the third is the same frame rescanned on 31 October after a month of work on the backend.

The plustek8200i backend on GitHub The original two-driver page
Read the columns carefully. The genesys raw data from October no longer exists, so that column cannot be re-converted — it is the published JPEG, and so is the October column beside it. Both were converted by the negative pipeline as it stood in October, which has itself changed since. So the tone and colour shift between the October and the current column is mostly the converter, not the backend. What the backend alone did is isolated in section 4, where two of our own raws taken a day apart go through one and the same conversion.

The October and genesys timings are single runs, as published. Ours today is the spread of repeat runs taken for this page — wide enough that the honest reading is that the backend is no slower than it was, not that it got faster.

What this means. Genesys's window sits 1.06 mm to the left of ours, a constant at both resolutions. It leaves a 1.2 mm dark strip on the left and runs up against the film's right edge. Its top and bottom margins are only 0.2–0.45 mm and drift with resolution (ours stay at ~1.6 mm), so a small positioning error would clip the picture.

3 · Detail at 3600 dpi

The same three crops as the original page, now with the current scan beside them. No resampling happens at 3600 dpi in any version of our backend, so this is the one place where our two columns should look alike — and they do.

@@CROPS@@
Sharpness is a wash; colour is not. All three resolve the same detail, and the dust specks line up across every column. What separates them is colour balance: genesys's raw data renders greener and brighter, which comes from each backend's white balance and exposure of the raw, not from lost detail. Between our own two columns the shift is the converter, not the scan.

4 · What the backend alone changed

Two of our own raws — 30 and 31 October, the holder untouched between them, matching to ncc 0.998 — put through one and the same conversion. Nothing here is the converter.

Vertical detail: a box average replaced by Lanczos-3

The sensor samples about 2.6× finer along the scan direction than across it, so every resolution below the hardware line rate arrives with surplus lines to squash away. That was a box average, which attenuates detail well below the new Nyquist and still passes enough above it to alias. Below: the wall's mortar courses at 600 dpi, where a squash of 2.48 applies, at 3× nearest-neighbour so the pixels are visible.

The infrared plane: per-column flat field

The colour pass is shading corrected in hardware; the infrared pass cannot be, because DVDSET clips IR, so the whole calibration was skipped and one scalar stood in for it. Every sensor column kept its own offset. The two calibration strips needed to fix it were already being read and discarded. These figures are from a different negative — a visibly dusty, scratched frame, the one that exposed the problem; the tulip frame above was scanned without infrared. The random noise was never the problem. Because dust detection thresholds on a multiple of that spread, a small speck sat under the bar: at the default sensitivity the pipeline found 28% of 2–4 px specks and now finds 98%. End to end, defect visibility above a clean-site control falls by 97.8% where it fell by 59.2% before, with high-frequency detail elsewhere moving +0.08%. Genesys exposes no infrared channel at all, so none of this is available through it.

How this was made. One frame, one OpticFilm 8200i (USB 07b3:1825). The genesys and October columns are the JPEGs published on 2026-10-09 in the original comparison, re-used unchanged: genesys was upstream SANE master (415217c5) with its GL128 command set, ours was plustek8200i at 750d632. The current column is plustek8200i at c77b4d2, scanned 2026-10-31 and converted with nv-cli render, no dust removal, the backend's own film-base estimate. Section 4 compares our raws of 30 and 31 October through that same conversion, so the backend is the only variable there. The crops were located by cross-correlating the published images against the new scan (ncc 0.91–0.99); the whole-frame views use the measured holder offset. Single scanner, single frame, so treat this as a worked example rather than a benchmark.

Where to go next. The backend, build instructions and the scripts that produced these images live in the OpticFilm8200i-sane repository. Upstreaming is tracked in SANE backends work item #886. The two-driver page this extends is here.
